Privacy Policy — Yes Plate

Last updated: 20 September 2026

The short version

Everything you enter into Yes Plate stays on your phone. There is no account to create, we run no servers that store your data, and we have no way to read what you record. That includes the health conditions you select — the most sensitive thing the app knows about you.

One thing to know: your phone may keep its own backup of the app's data in your Google or Apple account. That is the operating system's doing, not ours, and it goes to your account — but you should know it happens. See Your phone's own backup below.

What the app stores on your device

  • Your name and the icon you choose
  • Your language
  • The health conditions you select
  • Body details used to calculate your daily targets: sex, age, height, weight and activity level
  • Your body-composition goal, target weight and timeframe
  • Daily nutrient limits you enter yourself
  • Recipes you mark as favourites
  • Recipes you mark as cooked, and recipes you have opened
  • Your food diary: what you cooked, what you ate, when, and how much

All of it lives in the app's private storage on your device. Uninstalling the app deletes it.

Under EU data protection law, information about your health is a special category of personal data. The app's answer to that is not to collect it centrally at all.

What leaves your device

One thing: a check for app updates.

When you open Yes Plate, it asks Expo's update service (u.expo.dev) whether a newer version of the app's code is available. That request contains:

  • the platform (Android or iOS) and the app's version identifiers
  • a random identifier the app generates on first launch and keeps in its own storage. It is used only to deliver updates, is not derived from your phone's hardware, and is gone when you uninstall the app.
  • the IP address that any internet request carries

It does not contain your name, your health conditions, your body details or your diary. Expo's handling of that service is covered by their own privacy policy: https://expo.dev/privacy

Your phone's own backup

Android and iOS both back up app data by themselves. On Android this is Auto Backup, which copies the app's data to your Google Drive; on iPhone it is part of your iCloud backup. It happens at the operating system's level, without the app asking for it, and the copy lands in your own account — not with us. We never see it and cannot read it.

We leave it switched on deliberately. A lost or broken phone is the likeliest way to lose months of records, and this is the one safety net that works without you having to remember anything.

You can turn it off. On Android it is in Settings → Google → Backup; on iPhone, in Settings → your name → iCloud. Turning it off means a new phone starts empty unless you have saved a file yourself.

Saving a file yourself

You can also save a backup file from within the app. The app writes the file and hands it to your phone's own sharing menu — you choose where it goes. From that moment the file is in your hands, not ours.

The file contains everything listed above, including your health conditions. Keep it somewhere private. A messenger or a shared drive is not a good home for it.

What the app does not do

  • No accounts, no sign-in
  • No analytics, no advertising, no tracking
  • Nothing is sold, shared or sent to anyone
  • No access to your contacts, photos, location, camera or microphone

Children

Yes Plate is not aimed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect anything from them.

Your rights

Because your data never reaches us, there is nothing for us to hand over, correct or delete on your behalf. You control it directly:

  • See it — it is all in the app.
  • Change it — in the app.
  • Delete it — uninstall the app, or restore an earlier backup.

Not medical advice

Yes Plate offers general ideas, not medical advice. Your restrictions are set by your doctor, and the nutrition figures are estimates. The full disclaimer is in the app.

Changes to this policy
